Abstract

How can a 3D reconstruction system acquire and retain useful information to understand the geometry of a scene from partial views under a limited computation budget? Existing active view acquisition methods typically estimate uncertainty over observed or instantiated geometry, limiting their ability to reason about unseen structure, while long-horizon reconstruction methods often retain redundant observations.

We introduce Matisse, a training-free framework that unifies active reconstruction and keyframe selection using evidence from a pretrained generative 3D model. Matisse estimates Evidential Uncertainty from cross-attention evidence associated with 3D latent tokens and derives Evidential Information Gain to guide view acquisition and keyframe selection. Occlusion-aware, object-balanced aggregation supports multi-object scenes, while uncertainty propagation through intermediate latents avoids full reconstruction during planning.

Matisse reduces Chamfer distance by 12.7%, 3.8%, and 9.2% on GSO30, YCB-V, and Replica, respectively, relative to the best baseline on each dataset. On GSO30, it achieves a 1.50× end-to-end speedup over the best active reconstruction baseline with the same backend. For keyframe selection, Matisse achieves 18.3% lower Chamfer distance than Stream3D and approaches convergence using roughly 20% as many views.

Method at a glance

How Matisse works

Matisse interprets view-to-token association weights probabilistically. Evidence at each aligned 3D token gives its posterior uncertainty; candidate cameras are ranked by how much uncertainty they are expected to remove.

Probabilistic model and experimental settings

The noisy latent zτ uses denoising time τ, distinct from observation step t; θ denotes the pretrained generator parameters. The Gaussian derivation is understood at fixed denoising time. Its prior mean is μ0 ∈ ℝd, σ2 > 0 is a shared variance scale, I is the d × d identity matrix, and α > 0 is prior pseudo-evidence. A view with zero evidence for a token contributes no likelihood term.

GSO30, YCB-V, and Replica all use exploration penalty λ = 0.5 and prior pseudo-evidence α = 1. Active reconstruction uses five views per object on GSO30 and Replica, and five views per scene on YCB-V.

Keyframe selection

Lower reconstruction error with fewer views

Across 30 GSO30 objects on challenging 100-view casual trajectories, Matisse selects up to eight keyframes. Its fitted convergence plateau has 18.3% lower Chamfer distance than Stream3D and 21.9% lower than Evenly-spaced selection. It approaches convergence using roughly 20% as many views as Stream3D.
